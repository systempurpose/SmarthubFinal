To be completely candid, you are asking to bypass the fundamental security architecture of modern smartphones. Android and iOS specifically block a standard USB cable from transmitting the screen or accessing the system without explicit permission (like turning on USB Debugging or hitting "Trust this computer" on a working screen). This prevents thieves from simply plugging a locked, stolen phone into a laptop to steal data.

### The Invention: "Phantom-ADB" Software Payload

Since we cannot use hardware adapters or rely on existing software permissions, our invention must be a computer program that uses a low-level exploit to force the phone to talk to us.

**How the Phantom-ADB software would work:**

1. **The Handshake:** You plug the broken phone into your PC via a standard USB cable. The software recognizes the phone's chipset (Qualcomm, MediaTek, etc.).
2. **Virtual Keyboard (HID Injection):** The software temporarily mounts the PC as a virtual USB keyboard. It prompts you to type your phone's PIN on your computer keyboard. It fires those keystrokes over the USB cable to blindly unlock the phone's lock screen.
3. **The RAM Exploit:** Because USB debugging is off, the software uses a bootloader-level exploit (similar to tools used by data recovery labs or law enforcement). It injects a tiny, temporary mirroring protocol directly into the phone's working memory (RAM).
4. **The Override:** This injected script forcefully routes the phone's frame buffer (the video memory that usually goes to the screen) out through the USB data lines to your PC, completely bypassing the Android operating system's normal security checks. You now see your UI on your monitor.

### The Reality Check: What you can do today

Without inventing a zero-day exploit, a standard USB cable alone will not give you a visual UI on your computer if USB debugging is disabled. However, here are the strictly "USB cable only" workarounds you can attempt:

**1. Blind File Recovery (If previously trusted)**
If you have connected this phone to this specific computer before, the PC might already be a "trusted" device.

* Plug the phone into the PC.
* Turn the phone on.
* If you know your PIN, imagine the keypad on the broken glass and carefully try to tap it out to unlock the device.
* If successful, the phone's internal storage might automatically mount in your computer's File Explorer, allowing you to drag and drop your photos and documents (even though you still can't see the phone's UI).

**2. The Audio UI Bypass (TalkBack/VoiceOver)**
If you can't *see* the UI, you can force the phone to *tell* you what's on it.

* Plug the phone into your PC to keep it charged.
* Try to blindly unlock the phone.
* Press and hold both **Volume Up and Volume Down** buttons simultaneously for about 3 to 5 seconds. On most modern Androids and iPhones, this triggers the accessibility screen reader (TalkBack on Android, VoiceOver on iOS).
* If activated, the phone will speak aloud whatever you touch on the black screen. You can use this audio feedback to slowly navigate to Settings -> About Phone -> tap the Build Number 7 times, and blindly enable USB debugging!

**3. Pre-existing Wireless Tools**
If you ever previously set up **Windows Phone Link** (for Android) or **Samsung DeX on PC**, plugging the phone in and turning it on might trigger these apps to automatically connect to your PC if the phone joins your home Wi-Fi network.
